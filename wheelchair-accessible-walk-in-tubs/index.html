How transfer tubs work

A bath you can get into from your wheelchair

A regular tub asks you to stand, step and lower yourself down. For a wheelchair user, that can be hard or unsafe.

A transfer tub changes the order. You never have to stand in the tub. You sit first. Then you move your legs.

The door is wide and shaped like an L. It swings out into the room. The seat sits right by the opening. You pull your wheelchair up close. You slide across onto the seat. Then you swing your legs over the low edge and inside.

Close the door. Fill the tub. Enjoy a warm soak. When you are done, the water drains. You open the door and slide back to your chair.

Many people who do not use a wheelchair like transfer tubs too. Sliding onto a seat can be easier than stepping over a threshold, even a low one.

Step by step

How a transfer into the tub works

Every body is different. An occupational therapist can teach the safest method for you. Here is the general idea.

  1. Line up and lock

    Open the door fully. Roll your wheelchair beside the seat. Lock the brakes. Move the armrest and footrests out of the way if yours allow it.

  2. Slide and sit

    Use the grab bar and the tub edge for support. Slide across onto the seat. A transfer board can help if your therapist suggests one.

  3. Legs in, door closed

    Lift or swing your legs inside. Close and latch the door. Now fill the tub and relax.

Safety first: practice transfers with a therapist or caregiver before you bathe alone. Ask about grab bar placement outside the tub too. We can place bars where your hands need them.

Pick the right one

How to choose a transfer tub

Seat width

Start here. The seat must feel secure. Seats run from 22 inches on the Transfer30 and Transfer60 to 29 inches on the TransferXXL. If you use a wide wheelchair, a wider seat often feels better.

Tub length

Taller bathers want more legroom. The Transfer60 is 60 inches long. The TransferXXL is 55 inches. The others are 52 inches.

Tub width

Wider tubs give more room inside but take more floor space. The Transfer26 is just 26 inches wide for tight rooms.

Space for the chair

Your wheelchair needs room to pull up beside the seat. The outward door needs room to swing open. We measure both.

The path into your home

The tub must fit through your doors and halls. Wide models need a clear path. We check it before you order.

Hot water and jets

Bigger tubs hold more water. We check your water heater. If you want jets, we plan the power they need.

For caregivers

Easier bathing for the people who help

Helping someone bathe in a regular tub is hard work. You bend, lift and reach over a tall wall. That can hurt your back and put your loved one at risk.

A transfer tub helps both of you.

  • The wide open side lets you stand close.
  • Your loved one sits the whole time.
  • A handheld shower lets you rinse hair and skin easily.
  • Grab bars give your loved one something solid to hold.
  • The door stays closed and sealed while the tub is full.

Many of our calls come from adult children and spouses. If that is you, we are glad to talk through options with you and your loved one together.

Florida homes

Transfer tubs in Florida homes and condos

Florida bathrooms come in every shape. Beach condos. Ranch homes on slabs. Villas in 55+ communities. Each one needs a little planning.

Condos and HOAs

Many associations want approval before a bathroom remodel. Some limit work hours or elevator use. Ask early so install day goes smoothly.

Slab foundations

Many Florida homes sit on concrete. Moving a drain means cutting the slab. So we match the tub's drain side to your current drain when we can. Transfer tubs come in left and right drain versions.

Upstairs bathrooms

A full tub with a bather is heavy. For upper floors, we look at the floor and the filled weight of the tub first.

Humidity

Florida air is damp. A warm bath adds more moisture. A good exhaust fan helps protect your walls and fight mildew.

Hurricane season

Storms from June through November can delay deliveries and work. We plan around the weather and keep you updated.

What to look for

Safety features that matter for wheelchair users

Not every feature is a gimmick. These are the ones that make a real difference when you transfer from a chair.

A wide door opening

More room means an easier slide. Ella transfer doors are 36 inches wide.

A seat near the door

The shorter the slide, the safer the transfer. Transfer seats sit right by the opening.

A secure latch

The door must stay sealed while the tub is full. Ella transfer doors use a 2-latch gear and shaft lock.

Grab bars in reach

You need something solid to hold as you shift your weight. We can add bars outside the tub too.

A handheld shower

Rinse from the seat without reaching or twisting. It helps caregivers as well.

Easy controls

Large, simple controls near the seat help hands with arthritis or weak grip.

Temperature protection

Some wheelchair users have reduced feeling in their legs. Ask about an anti-scald valve that limits water temperature.

A fast drain

You stay seated while the tub empties. Ella's gravity-driven dual drain helps shorten the wait.

Questions and answers

Wheelchair accessible walk-in tub FAQ

What is a wheelchair accessible walk-in tub?

It is a walk-in tub made so a wheelchair user can get in without stepping. Most are transfer tubs. They have a wide door that swings out and a seat near the opening. You slide from your wheelchair onto the seat, then swing your legs in.

How wide is the door on an Ella transfer tub?

Ella transfer tubs use a 36-inch wide L-shaped door that swings outward.

Which Ella transfer tub has the widest seat?

The Ella TransferXXL has the widest seat at 29 inches. It is 36 inches wide and 55 inches long.

Can I roll my wheelchair into a walk-in tub?

No. A walk-in tub holds water, so it has walls and a door. You transfer from the wheelchair onto the seat. If you need to stay in your wheelchair, a roll-in shower is the better choice.

Is a transfer tub or a roll-in shower better for a wheelchair user?

A transfer tub is great if you can slide onto a seat and want to soak. A roll-in shower is better if you cannot transfer or a caregiver needs to stand beside you. We install both and help you choose.

Can a caregiver help with a transfer tub?

Yes. The wide outward door gives a caregiver room to stand close and help with the transfer. Talk with an occupational therapist about safe transfer techniques.

Do transfer tubs need extra floor space?

Yes. The outward swing door needs clear space to open fully, and a wheelchair needs room to pull up beside the tub. We measure your bathroom to make sure it works.

Will a transfer tub fit my current tub space?

Often. The Transfer60 fits a standard 60-inch space. The Transfer26, Transfer30 and Transfer32 are 52 inches long. We measure your space, doorways and drain side first.

Are there walk-in tubs for bariatric or very tall bathers?

Yes. The TransferXXL is a bariatric transfer tub with a 29-inch seat. The Big4One is built for bathers up to 7 feet tall, and the ShaK is 36 by 72 inches.

Which brands make wheelchair accessible walk-in tubs?

We install wheelchair accessible tubs from Ella, RANE by Bestbath and Meditub, and we sell Hydro Dimensions tubs too. Options include the Ella Transfer series, the RANE Mediterranean RB14 and the Meditub 3060WCA and 2953WCA.

Who makes your roll-in showers?

Bestbath is our top choice for roll-in showers. Bestbath makes its products in Caldwell, Idaho and Sparta, Tennessee, and our handicap showers include a 30-year warranty on the entire shower.

Does Medicare pay for a wheelchair accessible tub?

Original Medicare generally does not pay for walk-in tubs. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer bathroom safety benefits. Veterans may qualify for the VA HISA grant. Florida counties may offer SHIP repair help for accessibility work.
