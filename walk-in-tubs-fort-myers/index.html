Lee County

Rebuilding with accessibility in mind

Hurricane Ian made landfall in Lee County in September 2022, and later storms added more damage in parts of Southwest Florida. Many homes in Fort Myers, Cape Coral, Fort Myers Beach, Sanibel and Bonita Springs have had walls opened, floors replaced and bathrooms rebuilt.

If you are already repairing a bathroom, it can be the right moment to make it safer for the long term: a walk-in tub instead of a standard tub, a roll-in shower instead of a curb, grab bar blocking in the walls. It can also be the moment when flood rules matter most. This page covers both.

Flood rules

Understanding Lee County's 50 percent rule

Under floodplain rules tied to the National Flood Insurance Program, a building in a special flood hazard area that undergoes substantial improvement, generally when the cost of improvements reaches 50 percent of the building's value, has to be brought into compliance with current flood requirements. That can include elevating the structure.

Lee County's published guidance treats improvements cumulatively over a five-year period, so permitted work from recent years counts toward the total. The county also notes that a building whose lowest floor, including its bathrooms and mechanical equipment, is already above the required elevation may be handled differently. Cities such as Cape Coral, Fort Myers and Fort Myers Beach apply their own versions of these rules.

What this means: before you add a bathroom project to a list of storm repairs, ask your building department how much "improvement budget" your home has left.

Bring these to the building department

  • Your address and parcel number
  • Your elevation certificate, if you have one
  • A list of permits pulled in the last five years
  • The written quote for the bathroom work

Rebuild smart

Designing a rebuilt bathroom for the long haul

If storm repairs have your bathroom down to the studs, a few choices now can keep you in your home longer without another remodel later. None of these require a walk-in tub on day one, but they make one easy to add.

Backing for grab bars

Ask for blocking inside the walls around the tub, shower and toilet so bars can be added anywhere later.

A wider doorway

A wider bathroom door makes room for a walker or wheelchair. It is far easier to frame now than to widen later.

A curbless or low-curb shower

If the floor is open, a low or no-threshold shower base is simpler to set now than after tile goes down.

Valve and controls placement

Controls reachable from a seat, and a handheld shower on a slide bar, help bathers of every age.

Moisture-resistant materials

Ask your contractor about materials that handle moisture well, especially if your home is in a flood zone.

Lighting and outlets

Bright, even lighting and GFCI-protected outlets placed for future needs cost little during a rebuild.

Cape Coral

Cape Coral canal homes

Cape Coral is laid out around hundreds of miles of canals, and most homes are single-story concrete block on slabs, many built from the 1970s through the 2000s. For a walk-in tub, that usually means a straightforward alcove swap with one key detail: match the drain side so the slab stays intact.

Pool homes

Many Cape homes have a pool bath that opens to the lanai. Wet feet and tile are a slip risk, so a non-slip floor and a grab bar by the door are worth adding.

Older water heaters

Deep walk-in tubs need plenty of hot water. If your heater is original or small, plan for it in the budget.

Hurricane-season timing

Storms can delay shipping and crews. Leave room in the schedule from June through November.

Humidity

Make sure the bathroom fan vents outside, not into the attic.

Community living

55+ and golf communities in Estero and Bonita Springs

South Lee County has many gated, age-restricted and golf communities. Expect gate registration for contractors, HOA rules about trucks and dumpsters, and, in condo buildings, an alteration application with license and insurance documents.

Many residents are seasonal. If you are here only in winter, plan by phone and photos over the summer and schedule installation for early in your stay.

Questions and answers

Fort Myers walk-in tub FAQ

Does Lee County count a bathroom remodel toward the 50 percent rule?

For homes in special flood hazard areas, Lee County's guidance counts permitted improvements cumulatively over five years toward the substantial improvement threshold. Ask the building department how your project would be counted before you schedule it.

Is now a good time to add a walk-in tub if I am already doing storm repairs?

It can be, because walls and floors may already be open. Just confirm how the extra cost affects any flood-rule threshold for your home first.

Who helps older adults in Lee County find services?

The Area Agency on Aging for Southwest Florida, based in Fort Myers. The Florida Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337 will connect you.
