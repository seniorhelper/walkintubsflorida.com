Walk in tubs Florida trusts

A safer bath, without the climb

We carry over 50 types and styles of walk in tubs including wheelchair transfer baths designed for ease of entry.

A regular tub has a tall wall. You lift one leg over it. Then you lower yourself to the floor. For many people, that is the hardest part of the day.

A walk-in tub fixes that. It has a door. You step over a low lip. You sit on a seat at chair height. You close the door and fill the tub around you.

When you are done, the water drains. You open the door. You hold a grab bar and step out on a textured floor. No climbing. No sitting on a wet tub floor.

That is why Florida families call us. Some call after a slip. Some call after a hip or knee surgery. Some call because Mom stopped taking baths and did not say why.

We listen first. We learn who will use the tub and how they move. Then we help you pick the right tub. And we install it for you, from start to finish.

Most people want the whole job handled. That is what we do best. We measure. We remove the old tub. We install the new one. We show you how it all works before we leave.

Is it right for you?

Who a walk-in tub helps most

A walk-in tub is a great fit for many people. But not for everyone. Here is an honest way to think about it.

A walk-in tub is often a good fit if:

  • Stepping over a tub wall feels shaky or scary.
  • Getting up from the floor of a tub is hard.
  • You love a warm soak and do not want to give it up.
  • Warm water helps your sore muscles or stiff joints.
  • You can walk short distances and step over a low lip.
  • You can slide from a wheelchair onto a seat with a transfer tub.

A roll-in shower may be better if:

  • You use a wheelchair all day and cannot transfer.
  • A caregiver needs to stand right beside you.
  • You rarely take baths and prefer to shower.
  • Sitting while the tub fills and drains sounds hard.

We install both. So we have no reason to push one over the other. We just want you to get the setup that fits your life.

Sizing tips

How to pick the right walk-in tub size

The best tub fits two things: your body and your bathroom. Here is how we help you get both right.

Start with the space

Most regular tubs sit in a 60-inch opening. Many walk-in tubs are 52 inches long. Some are 55 or 60 inches. A 60-inch tub can fill the whole space. A 52-inch tub may need a small filler panel.

Check the seat width

Seat width is about comfort. Ella seats range from 19 inches on the Petite and Mobile to 29 inches on the TransferXXL. Wider seats suit larger bathers.

Think about height

Taller bathers want more legroom. The Lounger, Transfer60 and Ultimate offer 60 inches. The Big4One is built for bathers up to 7 feet tall.

Measure the path in

The tub must pass through your front door, halls and bathroom door. Tight turns matter too. We measure the whole path, not just the tub space.

Match the drain side

Stand facing your tub. Is the drain on the left or right? Most walk-in tubs come in left and right drain versions. Matching your drain keeps the job simpler, especially on a slab.

Plan for hot water

Bigger tubs hold more water. More water needs more hot water. We check your water heater so your first bath is warm, not lukewarm.

Easy tip: send us a few photos of your tub and bathroom door when you call. It helps us plan your quote.

Life with your new tub

A simple, safe bath routine

After we install your tub, we walk through it with you. Here is the routine most people follow.

  1. Set your towel and robe within reach.
  2. Open the door. Hold the grab bar. Step in and sit down.
  3. Close and latch the door.
  4. Start the water. Check the temperature with your hand.
  5. Relax. Turn on the jets if you like.
  6. Open the drain. Stay seated until the tub is empty.
  7. Rinse with the handheld shower while it drains to stay warm.
  8. Unlatch the door. Hold the bar. Stand slowly and step out.

Easy care

  • Rinse and wipe the tub after each bath.
  • Use gentle, non-scratch cleaners on acrylic.
  • Keep the door seal clean so it seals well.
  • Follow Ella's cleaning steps for jet lines.
  • Run the bathroom fan during and after your bath.

When to call us

Call if you see water near the door, the latch gets hard to close, or the tub drains slowly after you clean the drain. Small fixes are easy when caught early.

Built for Florida living

What we check in Florida homes before we install

Florida homes have their own quirks. Heat, humidity, storms and condo rules all shape a good install. Here is what we look at.

Humidity and airflow

Florida air is damp. A deep, warm bath adds more moisture. A good exhaust fan that vents outside helps protect walls and ceilings. It also helps fight mold and mildew.

Slab foundations

Many Florida homes sit on a concrete slab. Moving a drain means cutting concrete. So we match the tub's drain side to your current drain whenever we can.

Condos and HOAs

Many condo and HOA communities want approval before a bathroom remodel. Some have rules about work hours or elevators. Check with your association early.

Upstairs and high-rise bathrooms

A full walk-in tub with a bather is heavy. For upper floors, we look at the floor and the tub's filled weight before we recommend a model.

Hurricane season

Atlantic hurricane season runs June through November. Storms can delay deliveries and work. We plan around the weather and keep you updated.

Power for jets

Jets and heaters need a proper circuit, usually a dedicated GFCI-protected one. A soaking tub needs no extra power. Ella's drain is gravity driven.

Water heater size

Deep tubs need lots of hot water. Ella lists a suggested water heater size for each model. We check yours so your bath stays warm.

Seasonal residents

Live up north part of the year? Tell us your dates. We can plan your quote and install around when you are in Florida.

Keeping a regular tub

Many homes have two bathrooms. Some families keep a standard tub in one and add a walk-in tub in the other. That works well for guests and grandkids.

Tile, panels and finish

Removing an old tub can expose worn tile or wallboard. We look at the walls around the tub so your new bathroom looks finished, not patched.

Grab bars outside the tub

Safety does not stop at the tub. A grab bar by the toilet or bathroom door can help too. Ask us where extra support makes sense.

Doorways and hallways

The tub has to fit through your home on the way in. We measure the full path. The Ella Lounger, for example, fits through 28-inch doorways.

Ways Floridians get help paying

Medicare: Original Medicare generally does not pay for walk-in tubs. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer bathroom safety benefits. Read Medicare's rules.

Veterans: the VA HISA grant is a lifetime benefit for medically necessary home changes. It pays up to $6,800 for a service-connected disability, or up to $2,000 for other eligible veterans. See the VA HISA page.

County SHIP programs: many Florida counties use state SHIP funds for home repairs. Accessibility work can qualify. Rules differ by county, so call your county housing office.

Taxes: IRS Publication 502 says some home changes made for medical care can count as medical expenses. Read IRS Publication 502 and ask a tax pro.

Plain-English terms

Walk-in tub words, made simple

Shopping for a walk-in tub comes with new words. Here is what they mean.

Threshold

The low lip you step over at the door. Lower is easier.

Transfer tub

A tub with a wide door and seat near the opening, so you can slide in from a wheelchair.

Inward and outward swing

Which way the door opens. Inward saves floor space. Outward gives a wider entry.

Dual drain

Two drain paths that help empty the tub faster. Ella's dual drain is gravity driven.

Auto purge

A short cycle after your bath that blows water out of air jet lines.

GFCI circuit

A safety circuit that cuts power fast if something goes wrong. Jetted tubs need one.

Anti-scald valve

A valve that limits how hot the water can get, to help prevent burns.

Bariatric tub

An extra-wide tub with a larger seat and door for bigger bathers.

Yes. Ella makes two-seat walk-in tubs, including the Tub4Two, the Escape and the Big4Two.

Will my water heater be big enough?

Walk-in tubs hold a lot of water. Ella lists a suggested water heater size for each model. We check your water heater during the quote and tell you if it may come up short.

Does Medicare pay for a walk-in tub?

Original Medicare generally does not pay for walk-in tubs. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer bathroom safety benefits, but coverage varies. Call your plan and ask before you buy.

Can veterans get help paying for a walk-in tub?

Veterans may qualify for the VA HISA grant for medically necessary home changes. It is a lifetime benefit of up to $6,800 for a service-connected disability, or up to $2,000 for other eligible veterans. You apply through VA.

Are there Florida programs that help with bathroom accessibility?

Many Florida counties use SHIP housing funds for home repairs, and accessibility work can qualify. Each county sets its own rules. The Florida Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337 can connect you with your local Area Agency on Aging.

Do I need condo or HOA approval?

Many Florida condo and HOA communities ask for approval before a bathroom remodel. Check your association rules before install day. We are glad to help you with the details they ask for.

Do jetted walk-in tubs need electrical work?

Yes. Jets and heaters need proper power, usually a dedicated GFCI-protected circuit installed to code. A soaking tub without jets does not.

Can I shower in a walk-in tub?

Yes. Most walk-in tubs include a handheld shower, so you can rinse while seated. Ella Lay Down models are made for both bathing and showering.

Do walk-in tubs come with a left or right drain?

Most do. Stand facing your tub and note which side the drain is on. Matching it keeps the install simpler, especially on a concrete slab.

Do walk-in tub doors leak?

A well-installed walk-in tub with a good seal should not leak. Leaks usually come from a dirty or worn seal, a door that is not fully latched or a tub that is not level. Keep the seal clean and call us if you see water near the door.

Are walk-in tubs hard to clean?

No. Rinse and wipe the tub after each bath. Use gentle cleaners that are safe for acrylic. If you have jets, follow Ella's steps for cleaning the lines.
