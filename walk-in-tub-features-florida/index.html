Walk-in tub features for Florida homes, explained simply

Jets, heaters, drains, doors. Some features change how you feel every bath. Others mostly change the price. Here is how to tell the difference.

One simple rule

Safety first, comfort second, extras last

Every walk-in tub we sell has the safety basics: a door with a low step-in, a seat at chair height, grab bars and a textured floor. Those are not options. They are why the tub exists.

Comfort features come next. A fast drain and a handheld shower make every single bath better. Jets and heat help many people with sore muscles and stiff joints. Extras like colored lights are nice, but rarely the reason someone is glad they bought a tub five years later.

Each feature that uses power or water adds cost, adds electrical work and adds something to maintain. Pick the ones you will use, and skip the rest without guilt.

Doors

Inward, outward or wheelchair transfer?

Inward swing

The door opens into the tub. It needs no floor space outside, which helps in small bathrooms. Water pressure pushes it against its seal. The trade-off is a slightly tighter path to the seat.

Outward swing

The door opens into the room. You get a wider, easier path in, and a caregiver has more room to help. You need clear floor space in front of the tub for it to swing. Many Ella outward doors use a multi-latch lock for a tight seal.

Wheelchair transfer

A wide door swings out and the seat sits near the opening. You slide from a wheelchair or bench onto the seat, then bring your legs in. Ella transfer doors are 36 inches wide and L-shaped. See our transfer tub guide.

How to choose

Stand in your bathroom and picture the door open. Where will your feet, a walker or a wheelchair go? If space is tight, inward swing often wins. If help or transfers are involved, outward or transfer doors usually do.

Time in the tub

Fill and drain speed matter more than you think

With any walk-in tub, you sit inside with the door closed while it fills, and you stay seated while it drains. That is how the door stays sealed. The faster both happen, the less time you spend waiting and the warmer you stay.

A fast-fill faucet moves more water per minute than a standard spout. A fast drain uses larger or multiple drain paths. Real-world times always depend on your home's water pressure and drain pipes, so treat factory numbers as best-case.

  • Hydro Dimensions says its standard faucet can fill a tub in 4 to 6 minutes, at up to 18 gallons per minute at 60 psi.
  • Hydro Dimensions says its Safe-T Drain can empty a tub in as little as 85 seconds.
  • Ella uses a gravity-driven dual 2-inch drain with four ports.

Stay warm while it drains: rinse with the handheld shower as the water goes down. It is the simplest comfort feature there is.

Warmth

Heated seats, backrests and inline heaters

Inline heater

A heater on the jet system that keeps bath water warmer during a long soak. It does not heat cold water from scratch. Your water heater still does that job.

Heated seat or backrest

Warms the surfaces you sit and lean on, which helps while the tub fills and drains. Popular with people who feel chilled easily.

Water heater check

A deep walk-in tub holds far more water than a regular tub. Ella lists a suggested water heater size for each model. We check yours during the quote so the first bath is not lukewarm.

Florida note

Florida's warm groundwater helps your water heater, but long, hot soaks still add humidity to a small bathroom. Plan for good ventilation.

Safety extras

Small features with a big payoff

  • Anti-scald valve: limits how hot the water can get, to help prevent burns.
  • Handheld shower: rinse from the seat, and a caregiver can help without leaning in.
  • Extra grab bars: outside the tub, near the toilet or by the door.
  • Seat height and width: chair height makes standing easier. Ella seats run from 19 inches on the Petite and Mobile to 29 inches on the TransferXXL.
  • Controls in reach: make sure you can work every control while seated.
Extras

Nice-to-have features

Some models offer extras such as colored lights (sometimes called chromotherapy), foot massage jets, extra-long or lay-down interiors and two-seat designs. These can make a tub feel special. Before you pay for them, ask yourself whether you would still want the tub without them. If yes, they are a bonus. If not, think twice.

Questions and answers

Features FAQ

Are jets worth the extra cost?

For people who use warm water to ease sore muscles or stiff joints, many find jets worth it. If your main goal is a safer way to bathe, a soaker tub delivers the same safety for less and with less to maintain.

Do walk-in tub jets get dirty?

Any system that moves water needs care. Ella air systems purge the lines after each bath. For water jets, follow the manufacturer's cleaning steps, usually a periodic cleaning cycle.

Can I open the door if I feel unwell during a bath?

The door is designed to stay sealed while the tub holds water, so it should not be opened until the water drains. That is one reason a fast drain matters, and why anyone with a health condition should ask their doctor about soak time and temperature.

Which features do most people use every day?

The low step-in, the seat, the grab bars, the handheld shower and a fast drain. Those are the features we suggest getting right before adding anything else.
