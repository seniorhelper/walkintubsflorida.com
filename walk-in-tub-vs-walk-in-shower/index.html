Walk-in tub, walk-in shower or roll-in shower?

Three good options. Three different bathers. Here is a plain way to match the bathing setup to the person, the bathroom and the budget.

Direct answer: Choose a walk-in tub if the bather wants to soak and can step over a low threshold and sit. Choose a walk-in shower if they stand comfortably but trip over tub walls. Choose a roll-in shower if they use a wheelchair or shower chair and need no step at all.

The short answer

Start with the person, not the product

Families often ask us which option is "best." The honest answer is that each one solves a different problem. A walk-in tub is for someone who wants to sit and soak. A walk-in shower is for someone who can stand or sit on a bench and wants to skip the tall tub wall. A roll-in shower is for someone who arrives in a wheelchair or shower chair and needs no step at all.

We install all three across Florida. That means we have no reason to steer you one way. Our job is to ask good questions, look at the room and tell you what we would pick for our own parents.

Option 1

When a walk-in tub is the right call

A walk-in tub makes sense when bathing is more than getting clean. Many of the Floridians we talk with grew up with baths and miss them. Warm water helps stiff joints. A seat at chair height means they no longer lower themselves to the floor of a tub.

The trade-off is time. The door must be closed before the tub fills, and you stay seated until it drains. Good faucets and drains shorten the wait, and a handheld shower lets you rinse and stay warm while the water goes down.

  • You can step over a lip of a few inches, or slide from a wheelchair onto a transfer seat.
  • You want to soak to the shoulders.
  • You want hydrotherapy jets or a heated surface.
  • You are comfortable sitting for a few minutes while the tub fills and empties.

See our walk-in tub features guide for jets, heaters, drains and door styles, or our wheelchair transfer tub page if a slide-over seat is the goal.

Option 2

When a walk-in shower is the better choice

A walk-in shower replaces the tub with a shower base that has a low curb, or no curb at all. It is the most common change people make when they simply want to stop climbing over a tub wall. There is no waiting for water to fill or drain, and the space feels open.

Good fit if

You can stand for a few minutes, or sit on a built-in or fold-down bench. You prefer showers. You want the quickest daily routine with the least to maintain.

Less ideal if

You truly miss baths, or warm water soaks are part of how you manage pain. In that case a walk-in tub may be worth the extra time each bath takes.

Make it safer

Ask for a handheld shower on a slide bar, grab bars placed where your hands go, a slip-resistant floor and a seat. These small details matter more than the tile color.

Think ahead

If a walker or wheelchair may be part of life later, a low or no-curb base and a wider opening can save a second remodel down the road.

Option 3

When a roll-in shower is the answer

A roll-in shower is built so a wheelchair or rolling shower chair can enter without lifting over anything. The floor slopes gently to the drain. A padded fold-up seat, grab bars and a handheld shower finish the job.

This is often the best choice for people who use a wheelchair all day and cannot transfer, and for families where a caregiver helps with every shower. The helper can stand inside the space instead of leaning over a tub wall.

Florida homes

Florida details that can tip the decision

Slab floors and the drain

Most Florida homes sit on concrete. A walk-in tub that matches your current drain side often connects with little floor work. A curbless roll-in shower may need the floor recessed or sloped, which can mean cutting concrete. Ask how your choice affects the slab.

Condo buildings

In a high-rise, the floor build-up for a curbless shower and the filled weight of a deep tub both matter. Your association may want plans first. We help with the details they request.

Humidity

A long, hot soak adds a lot of moisture to a small room. Whichever you choose, plan for an exhaust fan that vents outside. Our Florida climate guide explains why.

Seasonal living

If you split the year between Florida and up north, think about where you will bathe most and when the work can happen while you are here.

Questions and answers

Tub or shower FAQ

Is a walk-in tub or a roll-in shower safer?

Neither is safer for everyone. A walk-in tub removes the tall tub wall and lets you bathe seated. A roll-in shower removes every step, which matters most for someone who uses a wheelchair all day. The safest choice is the one that matches how the bather moves today and is likely to move in a few years.

Can I have both a walk-in tub and a roll-in shower?

Yes, if you have two bathrooms or a large one. Some families put a walk-in tub in one bathroom and an accessible shower in the other. That gives everyone a choice on good days and harder days.

Does a walk-in shower use less hot water than a walk-in tub?

Usually, yes. A deep walk-in tub holds far more water than a short shower uses. That is why we check your water heater before recommending a tub.

Which option is quicker to install?

It depends on your room. A tub that drops into an existing 60-inch alcove with a matching drain is often a simple swap. A curbless shower that needs the floor reshaped usually takes longer. Your quote will spell out the timeline.
