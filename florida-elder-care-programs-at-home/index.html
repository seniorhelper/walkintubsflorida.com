Florida elder care at home: the state programs, explained

CCE, HCE, ADI and the waitlists, in plain English.

Call 1-888-779-2284
Free, no-pressure quotes.Real people. Clear answers.

Direct answer: Florida's main state-funded elder care programs at home are Community Care for the Elderly (CCE), Home Care for the Elderly (HCE) and the Alzheimer's Disease Initiative (ADI), alongside federal Older Americans Act services. You start with one call to the Elder Helpline, 1-800-963-5337. Expect a waitlist: in September 2025, 65,667 enrollments were waiting for CCE alone.

The short version

Four doors to help at home, one phone number

When families search for "Florida elder care," they usually mean one of two things: paying for a nursing home or assisted living, or getting enough help to keep a parent safely at home. This guide is about the second. Florida runs several programs that send help into the home, and most people reach all of them the same way, through the Aging and Disability Resource Center for their county.

The programs differ in who they serve and what they pay for. CCE is the broadest. HCE supports a family that has an older relative living with them. ADI focuses on Alzheimer's caregivers. Older Americans Act money funds meals, in-home help and caregiver support through the Area Agencies on Aging. Medicaid's long-term care program is separate and has its own rules, which we cover in our SMMC long-term care guide.

The waitlists

How long are the waitlists for Florida elder services?

Long, and they are ranked rather than first come, first served. After screening, each person is placed on the Assessed Prioritized Consumer List with a priority rank based on need. People at higher risk are served first, and people already referred by Adult Protective Services move up. The snapshot below, from a statewide list dated September 25, 2025, shows the scale.

The practical lesson is to get on the list early, even if you are not sure you need services yet, and to call back if the person's situation changes. A fall, a hospital stay or a caregiver who can no longer cope can change the priority rank. Write down the date of every call and the name of the person you spoke with.

While you wait, the fastest help is often local and smaller: a county SHIP repair program, a volunteer grab bar program or a church group. Our SHIP home repair guide by county lists the local contacts, and the Florida Area Agencies on Aging directory shows which agency serves each county.

Bathing

Where does bathing help fit in Florida elder care?

Bathing is usually the first daily task that becomes hard, and it is the one families feel least comfortable helping with. The programs above approach it from two directions.

A person to help. Personal care and home health aide visits under CCE, or aide services bought with HCE subsidies, put a trained helper in the bathroom a few times a week. That works well when the bathroom itself is usable.

A bathroom that needs less help. When the tub wall is the problem, more aide hours only go so far. A low-step walk-in tub with a seat, or a walk-in shower with a bench, lets many people bathe with less hands-on help, which can stretch limited aide hours further. The HCE special subsidy for home accessibility modifications is the program feature most likely to help with that cost, though it is not designed to pay for a whole bathroom.

For the hands-on side, Aging Safely Baths has a practical guide on how to bathe an elderly parent safely, and our own Florida caregiver bathing guide covers heat, humidity and the routine.

What a bathroom change costs: Aging Safely Baths publishes $15,250 to $20,000 installed for a walk-in tub and $13,500 to $17,000 for a roll-in shower, after current promotions. Veterans may qualify for a VA HISA grant of up to $6,800 toward medically needed bathroom changes. Original Medicare generally does not pay. See paying for a walk-in tub in Florida.

Applying

How do you apply without losing time?

  1. Call the Elder Helpline, 1-800-963-5337, or your county's Aging and Disability Resource Center. Say you want a screening for in-home services.
  2. Have the basics ready: date of birth, address, Medicare and any Medicaid numbers, income and assets, current medications, and who lives in the home.
  3. Describe daily tasks honestly. If bathing needs help, say so plainly. The assessment ranks need, and understating it can lower the priority.
  4. Ask which lists the person is on and how to update the screening if things change.
  5. Ask about HCE if the older adult lives with a family member who provides care, and about ADI if dementia is part of the picture.
  6. Plan the bathroom in parallel. A written, itemized quote is useful to any case manager who asks what a change would cost.

No bathing product eliminates the risk of falls; products are designed to reduce risk when installed and used properly. This page is general information, not legal or benefits advice. Programs set their own rules and can change them.

Questions and answers

Florida elder care FAQ

What is the Community Care for the Elderly program in Florida?

Community Care for the Elderly (CCE) is a state program run through the Department of Elder Affairs for Floridians 60 and older who are functionally impaired. After an assessment it can provide services such as case management, personal care, homemaker and chore help, home health aide visits, respite, adult day care, home-delivered meals and emergency home repair.

Does Florida pay family caregivers who care for an older relative at home?

The Home Care for the Elderly (HCE) program pays a basic subsidy of $160 a month to support an older adult who lives with an approved adult caregiver. Some participants also get special subsidies for things like medical supplies, wheelchairs, ramps and home accessibility modifications.

How long is the waitlist for Florida elder services?

There is no single wait time. Florida keeps an Assessed Prioritized Consumer List and serves people by priority, not first come, first served. On September 25, 2025 it showed 65,667 enrollments waiting for CCE, 20,757 for HCE, 16,027 for ADI, 48,254 for Older Americans Act services and 40,307 for Medicaid long-term care. People can be on more than one list.

Can CCE or HCE pay for a walk-in tub?

Rarely in full. CCE's home repair service is for emergency repairs, not remodels. HCE special subsidies can include home accessibility modifications, so it is worth asking the case manager. Most families combine program help with other sources such as VA grants, county SHIP repair programs or savings.

How do I apply for elder care services in Florida?

Call the Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337 or contact the Aging and Disability Resource Center for your county. A screening and assessment decide which programs fit and where the person is placed on the prioritized list.
