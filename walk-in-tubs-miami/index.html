High-rise living

Planning a walk-in tub in a Miami condo or co-op

Condo bathrooms in older Miami towers are often compact, and many units have a standard 60-inch tub alcove. That is good news, because many walk-in tubs are built for that space. The extra work is mostly paperwork and logistics.

Association approval

Most buildings want an alteration application before any plumbing work. Expect requests for a description of the work, the contractor's license and certificate of insurance, and the work schedule. Co-op boards may ask for more. Ask management for the form early.

Elevators and loading docks

A boxed walk-in tub is large. Buildings usually require a reserved service elevator, padded walls and use of the loading area. Measure the elevator door and cab depth along with your unit's doorways.

Work hours and noise

Many associations limit work to weekdays and set quiet hours. Removing a cast iron tub is noisy. We schedule around the rules your building sets.

Weight and plumbing stacks

A filled walk-in tub with a bather is heavy. In towers, the floor and the shared drain stack matter. Keeping the drain where it is avoids touching common plumbing whenever possible.

Small bathroom? Compact models exist. The Ella Mobile is 26 by 45 inches, and the Petite is 28 inches wide. Our measuring guide shows what to measure.

Houses

Single-family homes, from Hialeah to Homestead

Away from the coast, much of Miami-Dade is single-story concrete block homes on slabs, built across many decades. For a walk-in tub, a slab means the drain location is the key detail. Ordering the tub with the same drain side as your current tub usually means no concrete cutting.

Older homes in neighborhoods like Coral Gables, Miami Shores and the historic parts of Coconut Grove can have original tile and plaster walls. Removing an old tub may expose wall damage, so we look at the surround before quoting and plan a clean finish.

For a household with one bathroom, plan the install day around it. We will too.

Coastal planning

Flood zones and storm season in Miami-Dade

Much of coastal Miami-Dade, including Miami Beach and other barrier island communities, sits in mapped flood zones. Local building departments apply floodplain rules to improvements in those areas. A bathroom upgrade is usually modest compared with a home's value, but if you are doing several projects or post-storm repairs, ask the building department how it counts improvement costs. Our climate guide explains the 50 percent rule.

Hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30 and can shift shipping and install dates. Humidity is high most of the year here, so we always check that the bathroom exhaust fan vents outside.

Local help

Senior and caregiver help in Miami-Dade

  • Alliance for Aging

    The Area Agency on Aging and Aging and Disability Resource Center for Miami-Dade and Monroe counties. Start here for in-home services, caregiver support and Medicaid long-term care screening. Reach it through the Florida Elder Helpline.

    Elder Helpline 1-800-963-5337

  • SHINE Medicare counseling

    Free, unbiased help comparing Medicare Advantage plans, including any bathroom safety benefits, through the Elder Helpline.

  • County housing repair help

    Miami-Dade and some cities within it run housing repair programs funded partly by the state SHIP program. Rules and waiting lists vary. Call the county or your city housing office and ask about owner-occupied repair.

  • Veterans

    Veterans can ask their VA provider about the HISA grant for medically necessary bathroom changes. See our paying for it guide.

Miami walk-in tub FAQ

Can I put a walk-in tub in my Miami condo?

In many cases, yes. Most condo bathrooms with a standard tub alcove can fit a walk-in tub. Expect your association to require an application, proof of license and insurance, and a reserved service elevator for delivery.

Do you have a showroom in Miami?

No. We work by phone and photos, then measure in your home and handle installation. Call 1-888-779-2284 to start.
