How Floridians pay for a walk-in tub

Most families pay out of pocket, but some qualify for help. This guide sorts real programs from wishful thinking, with official links for each one.

The honest picture

You will see ads that say a walk-in tub is "covered" or "free with Medicare." Be careful. For most people, Original Medicare does not pay for one. Some other programs can help certain people, and a few can help a lot. They each have rules, paperwork and waiting time.

Here is how we suggest families approach it. First, check the programs that fit the bather's situation. Second, start the paperwork before you schedule anything, because approvals can take weeks. Third, get a written quote you can hand to a program or a tax professional.

We are not a benefits office, and nothing here is legal, tax or medical advice. We are happy to provide the written quote and product details any program asks for.

Medicare

Original Medicare: generally no

Medicare Part B covers durable medical equipment that a doctor orders for use in the home, such as walkers and hospital beds. Bathroom fixtures that become part of the house are a different category. In practice, Original Medicare generally does not pay for walk-in tubs, and you should be wary of any seller who promises otherwise.

Medicare's own coverage pages are the best place to confirm what is and is not covered. If a doctor recommends bathing changes after a hospital stay or a fall, ask what Medicare covers for therapy and safety equipment, which can be different from the tub itself.

Watch for: "Medicare-approved walk-in tub" claims. Ask the seller to show you, in writing, the Medicare rule they are relying on.

Medicare Advantage

Medicare Advantage: sometimes partial help

Medicare Advantage plans are run by private insurers approved by Medicare. Since 2019, CMS has allowed these plans to offer more "supplemental benefits" that are primarily health related. A 2018 CMS memo lists bathroom and home safety devices as an example category, with items such as shower stools, handheld showers, grab bars and raised toilet seats, and says plans may also cover installation.

What that means for you: some plans offer an allowance or a benefit for bathroom safety items. A full walk-in tub is a much bigger item, and plans decide what they include, so coverage for one is far from guaranteed. Florida has many Medicare Advantage options, and benefits vary by plan and by county.

Ask your plan

"Do you offer a home or bathroom safety benefit? What items does it include? Does it require a doctor's recommendation or a care plan? Is there a dollar limit, and do I have to use specific vendors?"

Check during enrollment

Medicare Open Enrollment runs October 15 through December 7 each year. If a bathroom safety benefit matters to you, compare plans then.

Free counseling

SHINE: unbiased Medicare help in Florida

SHINE (Serving Health Insurance Needs of Elders) is Florida's free Medicare counseling program, run through the Department of Elder Affairs with trained volunteers. A SHINE counselor does not sell plans. They can help you compare Medicare Advantage options, including any bathroom safety benefits, and explain what Original Medicare covers.

Reach SHINE through the Florida Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337.

Tip: bring a list of your doctors and prescriptions to a SHINE appointment. Plan networks matter as much as extra benefits.

Veterans

VA HISA grants for home changes

The Home Improvements and Structural Alterations (HISA) grant helps eligible veterans pay for medically necessary changes to their home, such as improving bathroom access. It is a lifetime benefit, not a yearly one. The amounts VA lists are up to $6,800 for veterans with a service-connected condition (and certain other qualifying veterans), or up to $2,000 for other eligible veterans enrolled in VA health care. Confirm the current amounts with VA before planning around them.

How it starts

The process begins with a VA health care provider, who must prescribe the change as medically necessary. VA's prosthetics service then handles the application.

Timing matters

Do not start work before approval. VA generally needs to approve the project first, and starting early can affect payment.

What VA may ask for

Usually a prescription, a completed application, an itemized quote and sometimes photos of the current bathroom. We can provide the quote and product sheets.

Bigger needs

Veterans with certain severe service-connected disabilities may qualify for larger adapted-housing grants. Ask VA which programs fit.

Medicaid

Florida Medicaid long-term care

Florida's Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care program serves people who are eligible for Medicaid and who need a nursing-home level of care but want to stay at home. The Agency for Health Care Administration lists "home accessibility adaptation" among the home and community-based services the program provides.

That service is decided through the enrollee's plan and care plan, and there can be waiting lists to enroll. To be screened, contact your local Aging and Disability Resource Center through the Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337.

Questions to ask the plan

  • Does my care plan include home accessibility adaptation?
  • Which bathroom changes qualify?
  • Is there a cost limit, and who chooses the contractor?
  • What documentation do you need from the installer?
County programs

County SHIP housing repair programs

Florida's State Housing Initiatives Partnership (SHIP) sends state housing money to counties and some cities. Many use it for owner-occupied repair programs, and accessibility changes can qualify. Each local government writes its own rules, income limits and waiting lists. Help is often a deferred or forgivable loan secured by the home rather than a cash grant.

To find yours, call your county or city housing office and ask about "SHIP owner-occupied rehabilitation" or "home repair assistance." Florida Housing Finance Corporation, which administers SHIP statewide, publishes local contacts.

Taxes

The IRS medical expense deduction

IRS Publication 502 explains that capital expenses for home improvements can count as medical expenses when their main purpose is medical care. The deductible part is generally the cost minus any increase in the home's value. The publication also lists certain changes made to accommodate a disability, such as grab bars and modified bathrooms, that generally do not increase a home's value.

To benefit, you must itemize deductions, and only medical expenses above 7.5% of adjusted gross income count. A letter from a doctor explaining the medical need is wise to keep with your records. Please consult a tax professional before relying on any deduction.

Keep: the itemized invoice, proof of payment, the doctor's note and before-and-after photos.

Questions and answers

Paying for a walk-in tub FAQ

Will a Medicare Advantage plan in Florida pay for a walk-in tub?

A few plans offer bathroom safety benefits, but most focus on smaller items like grab bars and shower seats. Whether a full walk-in tub is included depends on your specific plan. Call the plan and ask for the benefit details in writing.

Can I use the VA HISA grant after the tub is installed?

Generally, VA wants to approve a HISA project before work begins. Talk with your VA provider and prosthetics office first, then schedule the install once you have approval.

Is a walk-in tub tax deductible?

It may be, in part, if its main purpose is medical care and you itemize. IRS Publication 502 explains the rules, including subtracting any increase in home value. Consult a tax professional about your situation.

Who can help me understand my Medicare options for free?

Florida's SHINE program offers free, unbiased Medicare counseling. Call the Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337 to reach a counselor.
