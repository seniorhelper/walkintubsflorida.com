How to measure for a walk-in tub

A walk-in tub has to fit the space, fit through the house and work with your plumbing. Ten minutes with a tape measure answers most of those questions before anyone visits.

Why measuring first saves time and money

A walk-in tub is built to order around a few fixed numbers: its length, width, height, door side and drain side. If any of those do not match your bathroom, the result is a return, a delay or extra remodeling work.

You do not need to be handy to get good numbers. A 25-foot tape measure, a phone camera and a notepad are enough. Measure in inches, write each number down, and take a photo of each spot you measure. Our specialists use these notes to suggest models that fit before anyone visits your home.

Tip: Measure twice, at the top and at the bottom of the space. Walls are not always straight, and the smaller number is the one that counts.

The checklist

Six things to measure or note

1. Length of the space

Measure wall to wall where the tub will sit. Many walk-in tubs are made for the same 60-inch alcove as a standard tub. Shorter spaces may suit a compact model.

2. Width, or depth from the back wall

Measure from the back wall to the front edge of your current tub. Standard tubs are often about 30 to 32 inches. Wide and bariatric walk-in tubs need more.

3. Drain side

Face the tub from where you would step in. A drain at your left is a left-hand drain; at your right, a right-hand drain. Choosing the matching side keeps the plumbing simple.

4. Path into the house

Measure the narrowest door, hallway and corner between the front door and the bathroom. The boxed tub must travel that path, so compare it with the tub's width and depth on the spec sheet.

5. Water heater size

Look for the gallon rating on the label of your water heater. Walk-in tubs are deep, and a small tank may not fill one with hot water. Note the size, and whether it is gas, electric or tankless.

6. Electrical panel space

Tubs with jets, a heated seat or an in-line heater usually need one or two dedicated GFCI-protected circuits. Take a photo of your electrical panel so an electrician can confirm there is room.

Florida homes

Building on a slab changes the plumbing math

Most Florida homes are built on a concrete slab rather than over a basement or crawl space. The drain for your current tub runs down into that slab. Keeping the drain where it is usually means a simpler install. Moving it means cutting and patching concrete, which adds time and cost.

That is why the drain side matters so much here. If you order a tub with its drain on the same side as your current one, the installer can connect to the existing pipe. Condos and upper-floor units have their own rules too, so check with your association before work starts.

Condo owner? Ask your association about work hours, permits and any approval forms before you schedule an install.

Questions and answers

Measuring FAQ

What size space does a walk-in tub need?

Many walk-in tubs are made to fit the same 60-inch by 30-inch alcove as a standard bathtub. Compact models are shorter, and wide or bariatric models need more room. Measure the length, width and height of your space and share them when you ask for a quote.

Which way is my drain side?

Stand outside the tub and face the long side you would step in from. If the drain is at your left, it is a left-hand drain. If it is at your right, it is a right-hand drain. Ordering the matching drain side avoids moving the drain.

Why does it matter that many Florida homes are built on a slab?

Most Florida homes sit on a concrete slab, so the drain pipe runs through concrete instead of an open crawl space. Moving a drain on a slab means cutting and patching concrete, which adds time and cost. Matching the tub to your current drain location keeps the job simpler.

Do I need to measure my doorways?

Yes. The tub has to travel from the street to the bathroom, so measure the narrowest door, hallway and turn on that path. Compare those numbers with the tub's width and depth on its specification sheet before you order.
